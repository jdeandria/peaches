# Product decisions

Resolved design questions, so the reasoning survives the conversation
that produced it. Change a decision here first, then the code.

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What do Peaches do in a matchup? | **Bring their own workouts.** A matchup is a week; everything each Peach logs that week scores toward it. There is no generated workout. (Was: one generated workout per matchup.) | Peaches train however they like, solo, and still compete. Logging is the habit; the game sits on top of it. |
| 2 | What does the app know about what a Peach can do? | **Nothing up front.** Capabilities and the workout generator were removed; what a Peach logs is what the app (and their Crates) learn from (#21). (Was: a global per-Peach "I can do this" list.) | With no generator there was nothing to feed. |
| 3 | Handicap between fitness levels? | **No handicap; raw totals.** Reps and weight moved are separate categories, so a lighter Peach doing more reps and a stronger one moving more weight both earn, and the Crate's vote (#19) decides how much each is worth. (Was: scoring relative to a shared prescription.) | Simple and legible. Revisit with per-category weekly caps or personal baselines if one style dominates a Crate. |
| 4 | Capability vs. equipment on the day? | **Moot.** With no generator there is nothing to prescribe; Peaches log what they did with whatever they had. | Superseded by #1 and #2. |
| 5 | When does a matchup close? | **Automatically**: the week runs Monday 00:00 to the next Monday 00:00 in the Crate's time zone, then stays open for `loggingGraceHours` (default 6) so a Sunday-night workout can still be submitted. Then it is scored and final. Owners and admins can still close early. Any member's client runs the sync on launch and refresh. | Deterministic and the same for everyone in a Crate wherever they live. |
| 12 | Server component? | **None.** Client + Firestore only. Opening and closing weeks are idempotent (scores are a pure function of the shared logs) so any device can do them and they converge. | Simplicity and cost. The honour system already accepts client trust. |
| 6 | Verifying logs? | **Honour system, with plausibility checks and Crate disputes.** Sets with impossible numbers (pace faster than a world record, 900 kg loads, 5,000-rep sets) are refused; anything else a Crate doubts it can strike by majority vote (#26). | It's a game among friends, but raw totals make a typo ("50 km" for "5 km") decide a matchup, so obvious mistakes are caught at entry. |
| 7 | A Peach in two Crates? | **One log counts in every Crate.** On submit a log is shared with each Crate the Peach is active in, and each Crate scores it with its own weights. (Was: two workouts.) | One workout, one log. Different Crates valuing it differently is the point of voting. |
| 8 | Joining mid-season? | **Wait for the next Season.** Participants freeze when a Season is scheduled. Logs still share with the Crate and show in its activity. | Round-robin schedules do not take late entrants without unfairness. |
| 9 | Byes? | Points-for recorded, no win or loss. Standings rank by win percentage (ties count half), then wins, then point differential, then points for. | Standard fantasy convention. "Play the Crate median" is a possible later option. |
| 10 | App identity | The PWA manifest `id` is the site root, so an installed copy is identified by its origin (`https://<host>/`). | A PWA has no package id; moving hosts means users reinstall, so pick the production domain before launch. |
| 11 | Auth and backend | **Firebase Auth** (Google, Apple, email) and **Firestore**, behind the existing repository interfaces. | Matches the Firebase Hosting choice; one console for web, Android and iOS. |
| 13 | Accent colour from the peach seed | **M3 *fruit salad* variant** of #FFE5B4 for accents (coral-peach primary, the seed's gold as tertiary); a hue-free neutral scheme for every surface role. | The seed's own tone-40 accent reads gold-brown, not peach; fruit salad keeps the same seed but lands on peach-fruit coral, matching the 🍑 icon. Neutral surfaces keep backgrounds clean grey and white. |
| 14 | Material 3 Expressive | **Adopt it, implemented in-house** in `src/app/ui/expressive/` on top of Angular Material's M3 theme: spring motion tokens, wavy progress, the shape library for avatars, a morphing loading indicator, press-morphing buttons, connected button groups, emphasized type. | Angular Material has no Expressive components yet. Owning a few small components keeps the look stable and makes swapping to official ones a contained change when they ship. |
| 15 | Weight logging | Every set of an exercise that **takes a load** records its weight. Stored in **kg**, shown and dialled in the Peach's units (2.5 kg / 5 lb a step). The set logger starts from the last time that exercise was logged (every set: reps, weights, distances), so repeating a workout is a tap per exercise; editing a set's weight carries forward to later sets that held the same weight. Reps × load is the **weight moved** category. (Was: weight never scored; then only weights were pre-filled.) | With raw totals (#3) weight is one of the things a Crate can choose to reward. |
| 16 | Platform | **An Angular progressive web app.** One codebase served from Firebase Hosting, installable from the browser on Android, iOS and desktop, offline-capable via the Angular service worker and Firestore's persistent cache. No app-store builds. | One build and one deployment for every platform, no store review cycle, and updates reach installed copies on their next launch. |
| 17 | Time zones | New Crates and Peaches take the creator's device time zone rather than UTC. The demo is seeded in the visitor's zone so "this week" is their week. | Matchup weeks are defined in the Crate's zone (#5); UTC put evening workouts on the wrong date for most of the world. |
| 18 | How is a workout turned into points? | **Six categories with a par each**: run & walk (5 km), ride (20 km), swim & row (2,000 m), weight moved (5,000 kg), reps (150), active minutes (45). Points = `weight × amount / par`. Distance counts for an exercise's distance kind (from the catalog's equipment, or chosen for a custom exercise); active minutes are timed sets or the session's time on the clock (#27), whichever is more. Pars are fixed in code for now. | A par is "one solid session", so a weight of 25 means "a solid run is worth 25", which members can reason about without exchange rates between miles and pounds. |
| 19 | How do Crates decide what counts? | **One ballot for all the rules.** Each member splits 100 points across the categories (the Crate's weights are the mean of current members' ballots, rounded to whole points that still total 100) and votes on the plan rules (#24: bonus and penalty; each is the middle vote). Until anyone votes, defaults apply (20/10/10/20/20/20; 25% bonus, 50% penalty). A Season **freezes** all of it when scheduled; votes apply to the next Season. (Was: lineup rules: slots, pledge bonus, battle size.) | A fixed budget forces trade-offs and bounds any one voter's pull; a median does the same for a single number. Freezing means past weeks never re-score. |
| 20 | Matchup cadence | **Weekly.** Seasons are 1–12 weeks starting on a Monday, round robin as before. One open Season per Crate at a time. (Was: owner-picked exercise days.) | Logging is fluid, so daily rounds would punish rest days. Weekly is the fantasy-football rhythm. |
| 21 | What is shared with a Crate? | **Whatever its members log, automatically.** There is one way to add an exercise: log it. A Peach picks from their recent exercises, their crate-mates' and the catalog, or makes one up on the spot; when the log is shared, each exercise in it is shared with the Crate tagged with who logged it, so crate-mates can log it too. Nothing leaves the Crate; the global catalog is unchanged. (Was: a library of catalog favourites, custom exercises and routines, curated from three different screens.) | Several ways of adding exercises confused people. Logging is the habit; sharing as a side effect of it needs no curation. |
| 22 | Who can read a Peach's logs? | **Only the Peach**, drafts included. Submitting writes a copy under each Crate it is shared with (`crates/{id}/logs`), which that Crate's members read to score matchups, show activity and dispute. Editing a log rewrites its copies in the same batch (#28). | Firestore rules cannot check "shares a Crate with" on a query, but they can check membership of the Crate a copy lives under. Writing the log and its copies together keeps them from drifting. |
| 23 | Which week does a log count toward? | **The week it was started**: when the log screen was opened, restarting if the Peach reopens a draft that is still empty. It counts only if shared before that week closes. The Peach can correct the start later (#28). | Submitting at 00:30 on Monday still counts for Sunday's workout (within the grace period), and a log opened on Sunday but filled in on Tuesday is Tuesday's. |
| 24 | How do Peaches strategise around a matchup? | **They plan their week and commit to what it's worth.** A Peach puts workouts on the days of the week; in each Crate, the points that plan would earn under the Season's weights are their commitment. Reaching it earns `bonus`% of it on top; falling short costs `penalty`% of every point missed. Workouts all still count, planned or not. The Crate votes on both numbers (#19). See [SCORING.md](SCORING.md) §3. (Was: lineups of pledges in a few categories, with a bonus per pledge hit and a battle per category against the opponent.) | One decision, how big to plan, carries the strategy: a bigger plan is a bigger bonus but an easier miss, and the penalty makes over-promising cost. It rewards doing what you planned, which is the habit the app is for. Pledges and battles asked people to reason in categories and amounts; a plan is just workouts. |
| 25 | Can you see your opponent's plan? | **Its worth, live; never the plan.** Commitments follow the plan until the week starts (Monday 00:00 in the Crate's zone), then lock; crate-mates can read them throughout, and the Plan tab shows yours against your opponent's as both change. Only the number is shared: plans stay private. Commitments open a week before their week. The rules enforce who writes, when, and that a lock never moves. (Was: lineups were secret until the week started.) | Seeing the other side's number turns planning into a race, which encourages planning big; the penalty keeps that honest. Hiding the workouts keeps it a guess how they'll get there. |
| 26 | How are dubious numbers handled? | **Disputes, decided by the Crate's majority.** Any member can dispute one exercise or the session time of a crate-mate's shared log while its week is open. It is struck (stops scoring in that Crate) once more than half the Crate's current members vote strike; the owner counts as keep, so two-Peach Crates can't strike. Editing the disputed numbers ends the dispute. Undecided disputes at the week's close change nothing. | Plausibility checks catch the impossible, not the unlikely; friends are the best judges of each other's numbers. A majority of the whole Crate, not of whoever voted, keeps one keen voter from striking alone. |
| 27 | How long was the session? | **The log times itself**: the clock starts when the workout does (Start, or the first set logged; planning doesn't count), then runs while the log screen is open and visible, and pauses otherwise. Finishing is one tap, with no form. The time can be corrected afterwards. (Was: the Peach typed a session length on submit; then the clock started when the screen opened.) | Logging should be fast enough to do mid-workout; a clock that only runs on screen can't be inflated by leaving the app. |
| 28 | Can a finished workout change? | **Yes, by its Peach**: exercises, start time, session time and notes, or it can be deleted (with Undo), copies and all. The shared copies follow. A deleted workout stops counting in a week still open. A week that has closed keeps its result; the matchup page notes when its workouts were edited afterwards. (Was: submitted logs were frozen.) | Mistakes and forgotten logs happen. Closed results stay final so standings never shift under people. |
| 29 | How are sets entered? | **One set at a time, on dials.** Planning an exercise (or correcting a finished one) shows one set, picked from a strip of all of them, with its numbers on two dials (reps and weight, minutes and seconds, or distance and time): the rims of wheels whose hubs sit off the screen's edges, the way thumbs sweep. A swipe turns a dial a step at a time; a fling glides on, further than in proportion to its speed, so big changes are one flick; a tap types an exact value. Last time's set is marked on the dials. Next set / Add set (a copy of the one shown) sit beside Save. (Was: every set at once, each number with − and + buttons.) | Logging happens mid-workout, one set after another, often one-handed; a dial is faster than repeated taps, and showing one set keeps the numbers big. |
| 30 | Planning vs doing a workout | **Two modes.** Plan: pick exercises (or a saved routine) and set each one's target sets, pre-filled from last time. Log: Start opens a full-screen runner that takes the plan a set at a time: the dials start on the target, one big Done records what's on them (turn them first if the set went differently), an optional rest countdown follows (on by default, remembered per device, ±15 s, buzzes when up), and finishing an exercise asks for its RPE. A weight different from the plan becomes the working weight: the sets still to come at the old weight follow it. Sets can be skipped (a small button beside Done) and taken back; adding sets or exercises is for plan mode, and Back to plan keeps the progress. The dials take the whole width and the height left over, and their numbers grow with them. Finish is there throughout, and asks first if planned sets are left (they aren't logged). The plan lives on the draft log beside what was done; only done sets are entries, so only they score, and the plan is dropped on submit (Crates never see it; the rules refuse a shared copy that carries one). | Setting up and doing are different moments: one wants detail, the other one thumb and big targets between sets. Keeping the plan apart from what was done means scoring, sharing and disputes don't change. |
| 31 | Saved workouts | **Routines: a plan kept under a name, beside templates.** Templates are ready-made shapes (full body with dumbbells, upper and lower body in a gym, bodyweight, core, an easy run): each slot is a movement pattern, filled with a catalog exercise the kit allows, at random (plain exercises more often than tricky ones, no two of a family if it can help it), with reps, holds and distances varied a little and weights from last time. The mix changes daily and on Shuffle. Save the session's plan as a routine (saving under an existing name replaces it), start a later plan from one in a tap, delete with Undo. A routine keeps exercises and target sets only, not RPE or notes; up to 50 per Peach, private to them. (Revisits #21, which dropped a separate routines library: routines now come from, and go straight into, a session's plan, so there is still one way to add an exercise.) | A regular programme shouldn't need re-planning every time, and "start from last time" only covers one exercise at a time. |
| 32 | Finding an exercise | **Forgiving search over names and aliases, in a picker that holds still**: the sheet is a fixed height with the search at the top and the results scrolling under it, so typing never moves the box; Enter takes the top match, Escape clears before it closes, arrow keys walk the results, and on phones the keyboard waits for a tap on the search so recent exercises show first. Matching: case, hyphens, spacing and plurals don't matter ("pushups", "push-up" and "Push Up" match), every word must start a word of the name (or, four letters or more, appear in it), and names that start with the search come first, then aliases that do. | People type what they call it, mid-workout, on a phone. |
| 33 | Editing and removing workouts and exercises in a list | **Swipe, on touch screens.** Swipe a workout in your history (You → History) or on the Plan right to edit it, left to delete it or take it off the plan (a finished workout's page also has Delete); swipe an exercise (planned, or in a finished workout) right to edit its sets, left to remove it; a saved routine swipes left to delete. The row follows the finger over a coloured layer naming the action and acts past about a third of its width (or on a quick flick), otherwise springs back; removing slides it away and offers Undo, which puts it back where it was. On touch screens the row's edit and remove buttons stay out of sight until a keyboard or screen reader reaches them; with a mouse they show as before, and a mouse drag still selects text. | The buttons crowded every row on a phone, and swiping a list row is how phones do it. |
| 34 | How the app is laid out | **Three tabs: Plan (home), Crates, You.** Plan is the week: this week or next, how it's going, a strip per Crate matchup comparing your plan with your opponent's, then the days, each with its workouts to plan, start, continue or see done. Crates is crate stats: a switcher between your Crates, this week's matchup first, then what needs you, then Season, Activity and Peaches. You holds your history, stats and records, equipment and settings. Every screen orders things the same way: what needs you, what's live, what's next, what's done. docs/FLOW.md has the details. (Was: Train, Compete, Crates and Profile; before that one Today tab.) | Planning the week is now the game as well as the habit, so it's the home tab, and launching a workout from its day is the shortest way into the log. Matchups belong with the Crate they're in. |
| 35 | Where does a Peach train? | **Equipment locations on the Peach**: named places (Home, Gym, Travel presets) each with the equipment there, up to eight. A planned workout names one (or Anywhere): the exercise picker then lists what fits that kit (with a toggle for everything), and templates use only the kit that's there. Logging is unaffected. | Planning a week happens away from the gym; suggesting what's possible where you'll be saves swapping exercises on the day. Keeping it on the Peach (not per workout) means setting it up once. |
| 36 | What is a plan worth? | **What its target sets would earn**, scored exactly like a log, in a session as long as the plan suggests: two minutes for each untimed set (with its rest), a timed set's time plus a minute's rest, a distance's own time. One plan is priced separately in each Crate. Commitments are capped at 2,000 points. | Without a session time a plan would leave out active minutes and be trivially easy to beat; an estimate from the sets keeps hitting the plan about doing what was planned. |
